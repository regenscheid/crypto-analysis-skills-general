# Interpret a source, use a result, or verify a paper

Choose the work from the user's question. Citing a paper does not by itself
assign an audit of that paper.

| Question | Work owed | Suitable route |
|---|---|---|
| Does the paper say X? | Inspect the exact statement, revision, assumptions, and material qualifications | Source reading or literature extraction |
| Is the paper's claim X correct? | Check the argument or evidence that is supposed to establish X | `crypto-verify-claim`; `crypto-analyze-paper` for a whole-paper review |
| What follows from X for this mathematical question? | Check the ingredient's hypotheses and develop the additional inference | `mathematical-research-development` for a stated mathematical question |

These questions can occur in one assignment. Keep their conclusions separate.
An accurate transcription establishes an attribution, not the correctness of the
transcribed theorem. A sound new deduction can be conditional on a cited premise
whose original proof has not been independently audited here.

## Use established ingredients with explicit scope

Use an adequately sourced theorem with its exact assumptions and qualifications.
Check that the current objects satisfy those assumptions. Do not independently
re-prove every cited theorem, rerun every published experiment, or audit every
paper in its bibliography as a prerequisite to ordinary mathematical work.

A supplied, previously inspected statement remains usable when its revision,
scope, and dependencies match. Cite that provenance honestly; do not describe it
as a fresh source opening or a reproduction. Reopen or recheck the affected part
when there is a revised source, changed assumption, conflicting evidence,
suspected error, or an explicit verification objective. Merely beginning another
session is not a changed scientific dependency.

When an ingredient is uncertain, state the exact dependency. Work that proves
“if H, then C” can remain useful while H is unresolved. It does not establish C
unconditionally. A report can distinguish **relied on as a published result**,
**hypotheses checked here**, and **independently verified here** without making
every reference pass through the last category.

## Bound a paper-verification subtask

When correctness is the assigned question, retain rigorous checking. Identify
the source revision, exact claim, supporting argument or computation, suspected
gap if any, and the evidence needed to decide it. Audit the parts that support
that claim. A whole-paper audit and a check of one lemma are different scopes.

Independent verification challenges a specific error: a logical gap, wrong
input, transcription error, numerical instability, or inappropriate sampling
model. A separate derivation from the same specification can do that. Another
paper repeating the same unchecked premise cannot by itself do it.

Give a verdict on the argument actually reviewed. A missing step is unresolved
unless a valid contradiction defeats the stated claim. A proposed repair is
separate subsequent mathematical work; it does not silently change the verdict
on the original version.

## Use shared rules without expanding the assignment

This reference governs the distinction between source interpretation, use, and
paper verification. The [evidence reference](evidence-interpretation.md) governs
what an observation can establish. The [contribution reference](contribution-assessment.md)
governs originality language. Technique-specific assumptions remain local, and
the host owns execution permissions and project context. A specialist checklist
applies to the work actually assigned to that specialist; an associated citation
or skill link does not assign all of its procedures.
